Caught on paper

Four problems that become change orders.

Found before bid day, each one costs a question to your design team. Found during construction, each one costs a change order.

Conflicts

Two sheets that disagree

The finish schedule says one material and the floor plan says another. Both sheets get flagged and the question goes to your architect, so the price carries the answer.

Missing information

Details nobody drew

Equipment with no size, or a note pointing to a detail that isn't in the set. Found now, it's an RFI. Found in the field, it's a delay and a change order.

Gaps between trades

Work no trade carries

Electrical says by mechanical. Mechanical says by electrical. Every requirement is assigned to exactly one trade, and anything left over is settled before bid day.

Schedules

Priced by the tag

Every door, fixture and finish in a schedule is priced item by item. A bid that says "per plans" is where scope goes missing.

Lost time

Not every problem is a change order. Some just cost you time.

A question with a free answer can still stop the work while everyone waits on it. It may never become a change order, but you pay for the time: more loan interest, more insurance, and a later day to open or start collecting rent.

Straight answers

What owners ask about GC Level.

Does this mean no change orders? +
No. If you change the design, or we open a wall and find something no drawing could show, that's a change. It comes to you in writing, priced, and you sign it before the work happens. What goes away are the change orders that were already sitting in the drawings on bid day.
Why might your number be higher than another bid? +
Because it includes the work your drawings actually call for. Put the bids side by side and ask each contractor for their exclusions. We'll walk you through ours, line by line.
Who makes the calls, the software or you? +
We do. GC Level reads and flags. A Spire owner reviews what it finds, decides how each item is priced, and sends the open questions to your design team in writing.
What do you need from me? +
The full drawing set and specifications, as PDFs. If you're still in design, send what you have. Questions found early cost the least to answer.
We already have a contractor's bid. Can you check it? +
Yes. Bid leveling is part of our consulting work. We'll compare the bids you have against what your drawings call for and show you what's missing.
